# Mode Characteristics of Optical Fiber

## Aim
To study the mode characteristics of a fiber optic cable and observe the lower order Linearly Polarized (LP) modes.

---

## Equipments Required
1. LASER source (633 nm – 1 mW)  
2. Source-to-fiber coupler  
3. Single-mode fiber  
4. Fiber holding stand  
5. Opaque screen  

---

## Theory

The central spot carries 95% of the intensity for laser beams with a Gaussian profile.

\[
I = I_0 e^{-2(r / w)^2}
\]

where \( e = 2.718 \) is the base of the natural logarithm.

- An accepted definition of the radius of a Gaussian beam is at 0.135 times its peak value \( I_0 \).
- This radius is known as the **spot size**.
- The spot diameter is denoted by \( w \).

**Spot Diameter (d) in microns** is given by:

\[
d = f \times DA
\]

where:
- \( f \) = focal length of the lens (mm)  
- \( DA \) = laser beam full divergence angle (mrad)

For maximum coupling efficiency, the fiber core diameter must be greater than the spot diameter.

The numerical aperture (NA) is given by:

\[
NA = \frac{\text{Laser Beam Diameter (B.D.)}}{2 \times \text{Lens Focal Length (f)}}
\]

---

### Source-to-Fiber Coupler

The source coupler consists of two base plates:
- One base plate holds a focusing lens and a female connector receptacle.
- The other base plate mounts onto the laser.
- An O-ring is placed between the plates.
- Threaded screws interconnect the plates and allow angular alignment.
- Alignment is adjusted using a screwdriver.

---

### Modes and V-Number

- The number of modes propagating in an optical fiber depends on the **V-number**.
- If \( V < 2.045 \), only a single mode propagates (HE\(_{11}\) or LP\(_{01}\)).
- If \( V > 2.045 \), multiple modes propagate.
- For \( V = 4.91 \), four Linearly Polarized (LP) modes propagate through the fiber.

---

## Procedure

1. Place the optical breadboard on a flat table.
2. Fix the cylindrical head of the He–Ne laser source securely onto the breadboard.
3. Mount the laser to the fiber coupler with the base plate facing the laser output.
4. Switch on the He–Ne laser and align the beam centrally on the coupling lens assembly. Tighten the screws.
5. Check for back reflection from the rod lens of the coupler and adjust screws until the reflected spot coincides with the laser exit.
6. Use a white card sheet to confirm central alignment and make fine adjustments if needed.
7. Connect the multimode optical patch cord to the coupler and secure the other end in the fiber holding stand.
8. Observe the bright laser spot exiting the fiber and adjust the fiber tip height to approximately 50 mm above the paper.
9. Observe the multimode speckle pattern on the screen and refine alignment using the screws. Replace the multimode fiber with single-mode fiber.
10. For single-mode fiber, observe blur patterns with two, three, or four lobes by fine-tuning the coupler screws.
